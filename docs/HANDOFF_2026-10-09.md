# UIP — Macro Investment & Housing Timing System (MIHTS)
## Technical implementation and historical handoff
**Prepared:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Design/handoff; not a deployed or backtested UIP component  
**Target:** UniversalInvestmentPlatform (`dlockar1mtg/UniversalInvestmentPlatform`)

## 1. Executive purpose
MIHTS is a rules-based macroeconomic monitoring and decision-support module intended to connect national business-cycle conditions with (a) investment risk/opportunity, (b) household portfolio deployment, and (c) the timing of a primary-residence purchase. The user focuses on Wichita/Maize, Kansas, with Dallas/DFW as a secondary housing market, and a roughly three-year housing horizon. The intended asset universe includes large-cap equities, small-cap ETFs, Bitcoin, Ethereum, gold, silver, and liquid house reserves. MIHTS must inform, **not automatically execute**, trades or housing purchases.

Four distinct outputs must be displayed: (1) Composite Recession Stress Index (RSI, -1 to +1; negative = expansionary, positive = stress), (2) estimated 12-month recession probability (0–100%, **uncalibrated until backtested**), (3) Asset Opportunity Score (0–100), and (4) Housing Opportunity Score (0–100, metro-specific when data permit). Companion asset-class opportunity scores are proposed but experimental.

**Critical warning:** Earlier numerical readings and probabilities were judgment-based, with changing thresholds and inconsistent arithmetic. They are **historical analyst snapshots, not a reproducible, statistically validated series**. Preserve the original claims as provenance, but recompute the entire history from a frozen methodology and official point-in-time data. Do not silently promote historical estimates into certified signals.

## 2. Origin and evolution
### v1 — equal-weight Composite Recession Stress Index
The first RSI used eleven indicators: yield curve, M2 growth, payrolls, initial claims, unemployment change, credit spreads, manufacturing PMI, consumer sentiment, housing starts/permits, S&P 500 trend, and oil. Each was scored `-1` (expansion/support), `0` (neutral), or `+1` (stress). The original RSI was the simple average. Approximate interpretive bands were `<0` expansion, `0–0.2` late cycle, `0.2–0.4` deterioration, `0.4–0.6` recessionary stress, and `>0.6` panic. These are heuristic labels, not measured recession frequencies.

Examples of historical provisional thresholds: 10Y–3M curve >1pp = -1, 0–1pp = 0, inverted = +1; M2 YoY >6% = -1, 3–6% = 0, <3% = +1; payroll growth >150k = -1, 0–150k = 0, negative = +1; initial claims <220k = -1, 220–260k = 0, >260k = +1; HY spreads <3pp = -1, 3–5pp = 0, >5pp = +1; PMI >52 = -1, 48–52 = 0, <48 = +1; Michigan sentiment >70 = -1, 50–70 = 0, <50 = +1. Other factors relied on qualitative trends. These thresholds are **design history**, not final production specifications.

### v2 — weighted Composite RSI
System weights were explicitly accepted:

| System | Total weight | Components and within-system weights |
|---|---:|---|
| Labor | 30% | Payrolls 40%; initial/continuing claims 35%; unemployment 25% |
| Financial conditions | 25% | Yield curve 50%; credit spreads 50% |
| Real economy | 20% | Manufacturing PMI 60%; housing starts/permits 40% |
| Liquidity | 10% | M2 growth 100% |
| Market expectations | 10% | S&P 500 trend 100% |
| Consumer psychology | 5% | Michigan sentiment 100% |

Formula: `RSI = 0.30*L + 0.25*F + 0.20*R + 0.10*M + 0.10*E + 0.05*C`. Component and system scores are bounded to [-1,+1]. The six system weights sum to 100%.

**Unresolved design decision: oil.** Oil was an original v1 input and subsequently affected interpretations, but it was omitted from the accepted six-system v2 formula. Do not quietly insert it into RSI and mislabel the result v2.0. Proposed path: keep the accepted RSI v2.0 formula unchanged; add oil to the Asset Opportunity Score and a separately displayed `inflation_energy_shock` overlay. Evaluate a formally versioned RSI v2.1 with an explicit energy weight only after tests/backtesting.

### v3 — MIHTS four-output framework
The user then approved adding (1) recession probability, (2) asset opportunity, and (3) housing opportunity alongside RSI. Subsequent commentary called the overall system **MIHTS v3.0**; this is an architectural name, not proof that any implementation exists.

## 3. Scoring and model governance
- Store raw observations with units, series IDs, observation date, publication timestamp, retrieval timestamp, vintage/revision, geography, and source URL.
- Distinguish **observation date** from **as-of/report date**; never mix later revisions into historical backtests without labeling the test as revised-data.
- Normalize all indicators to documented bounded scores [-1,+1]; use deterministic thresholds/continuous interpolation, and publish thresholds and transformations in version-controlled config.
- For payrolls use 3-month trend and revisions; claims use four-week average plus continuing claims; unemployment use level, 3-month change, participation and optionally Sahm-style change; housing use starts, permits, single-family mix and revisions; yield curve use 10Y–3M spread and distinguish steepening due to falling short yields from rising long yields.
- Never treat a positive yield curve as automatically low recession risk: uninversion after inversion may itself occur near downturns. Likewise, low credit spreads are not a guarantee of future resilience.
- Keep the RSI formula deterministic, auditable and unchanged within a version. Report component scores and weighted contributions. Separate confidence/coverage from score.
- If inputs are stale/missing, mark stale, publish coverage and suppress a misleading headline when minimum requirements fail. Never replace missing observations with invented current values.
- All scores, thresholds, horizons and status bands must have configuration version and reproducible test fixtures.

## 4. Four output specifications
### A. Composite RSI [-1,+1]
Implement the accepted six-system v2 formula above. Negative values indicate expansion-supportive conditions; positive indicate stress. Interpretation bands remain provisional until historical distribution and predictive utility are studied. Show the weighted component table and month-over-month deltas.

### B. 12-month recession probability [0,100%]
**Do not use the historical 12%, 15%, 18%, 20% claims as calibrated probabilities.** Build a supervised time-series model with recession onset or recession occurrence within 12 months as a clearly defined target using NBER dates; test alternative horizons (6, 12, 24 months). Candidate predictors: labor trends, claims, yield curve including uninversion, credit spreads, PMI, and housing. Use expanding-window/walk-forward training, release lags, vintages, proper scoring (Brier/log loss), reliability plots, discrimination metrics, and comparison with naive base-rate and established benchmark models. Until validated, display `recession_risk_heuristic` with an explicit non-probabilistic label or hide numeric probability.

### C. Asset Opportunity Score [0,100]
Original proposed driver weights:

| Input | Weight |
|---|---:|
| M2/liquidity | 25% |
| Credit spreads | 20% |
| PMI | 15% |
| S&P 500 trend | 15% |
| Oil | 10% |
| Payrolls | 10% |
| Yield curve | 5% |

Define direction carefully: **a healthy economy is not the same as an attractive entry price**. An opportunity score should incorporate valuation, forward return expectations, drawdown, volatility, real yields, and horizon before it recommends 'strong buy.' The historical 80+ readings mostly captured supportive macro conditions, not demonstrated expected excess returns. Initially label this `macro_asset_environment_score`; do not use it to auto-rebalance or trade. For per-asset scores (large cap, small cap, BTC, ETH, gold, silver, cash), design separate risk/valuation mappings with provenance and backtests; previous per-asset numbers are illustrative only.

### D. Housing Opportunity Score [0,100]
The user approved a housing output, but weights were discussed in more than one draft. The latest explicitly presented breakdown was: mortgage rate 20%, starts 15%, permits 15%, inventory 15%, affordability 10%, labor 10%, regional prices 10%, credit 5% (100%). Earlier proposal was mortgage 25%, starts 20%, permits 15%, inventory 15%, unemployment 10%, credit 10%, regional prices 5%. **Freeze one specification only after documenting the decision**. The 20/15/15/15/10/10/10/5 breakdown is the latest working proposal, not a calibrated specification.

Build national macro context plus metro-specific Wichita/Maize and DFW scores. Incorporate Zillow ZHVI/ZORI, Realtor inventory, Census ACS income, local prices, listing time, price cuts, concessions where available, and a mortgage affordability calculator (rate, down payment, taxes, insurance, PMI, closing costs). Separate a good market from **personal readiness** (credit, reserves, debt-to-income, employment stability, down payment). Avoid treating weak starts as unambiguously buyer-positive: supply contraction can also worsen future affordability. Display an actionable watchlist of conditions, not an instruction to buy.

## 6. Data acquisition plan
Use official, reproducible sources, preferably APIs/CSV with no fragile scraping. Suggested mappings:

| Signal | Source / series candidates | Frequency / issues |
|---|---|---|
| 10Y Treasury, 3M, 2Y | FRED `DGS10`, `DGS3MO`, `DGS2`; Treasury daily rates | Daily; holiday gaps; basis-point conversion |
| M2 | FRED `M2SL` / Federal Reserve H.6 | Monthly, revisions and lag; compute YoY from levels |
| Payrolls | BLS CES `CES0000000001` | Monthly; use monthly level difference, revisions |
| Initial claims | DOL ETA / FRED `ICSA`, `IC4WSA` | Weekly; align to release date |
| Continuing claims | DOL / FRED `CCSA` | Weekly, reporting lag |
| Unemployment | BLS CPS / FRED `UNRATE` | Monthly; 3-month change |
| Participation | BLS / FRED `CIVPART` | Monthly |
| Baa yield | Moody's via FRED `BAA` | Daily; subtract comparable Treasury cautiously |
| High-yield OAS | ICE BofA via FRED `BAMLH0A0HYM2` | Daily; series terms/licensing |
| ISM manufacturing | ISM monthly report | Monthly; check data access/terms |
| Composite PMI | S&P Global | Licensing may restrict automation; optional |
| Consumer sentiment | University of Michigan / FRED `UMCSENT` | Preliminary vs final vintages |
| Starts/permits | Census New Residential Construction / FRED `HOUST`, `PERMIT`, `HOUST1F`, `PERMIT1` | Monthly; revisions, mix |
| Equity benchmark | Existing governed Stocks/ETF data or permitted market-price source | Daily; adjusted close, trend windows |
| Oil | FRED `DCOILWTICO` and Brent `DCOILBRENTEU` / EIA | Daily; spot vs futures distinguish |
| Mortgage rates | FRED `MORTGAGE30US` | Weekly |

FRED can be accessed using existing FRED credentials where authorized. Avoid introducing an unowned paid API dependency. Never commit keys; use GitHub Actions secrets / hosted environment variables. Verify licensing and redisplay rights for every feed.

## 8. Automation and release cadence
1. **Scheduled ingest:** daily business-day rates/credit/oil/market data; weekly claims and mortgage rates; monthly employment, M2, PMI, sentiment, starts/permits and local housing; annual income. Trigger additional runs on releases where possible.
2. **Ingestion validation:** schema, units, date ordering, duplicate keys, suspicious jumps, nulls, source freshness, revisions, and provenance; quarantine questionable data.
3. **Feature computation:** align to `as_of` and published availability; calculate trends, YoY, rolling averages and lags; preserve vintages.
4. **Model scoring:** deterministic RSI; other outputs versioned independently; publish components, score, coverage and changes.
5. **Historical append:** append immutable model run with version, manifest and execution timestamp; allow replay of past as-of dates.
6. **Governed publication:** publish only on successful validation to the same production mechanism as UIP; keep last-known-good with stale banner on failure.
7. **UI:** four KPI cards, score history chart, six-system contribution waterfall/table, data freshness, next releases, local housing view, and expandable rationale; clearly mark uncalibrated risk and experimental opportunity scores.
8. **Alerts:** configurable triggers for sustained claims acceleration, HY spread widening, curve transitions, PMI contraction, oil shock, mortgage-rate changes, and regional affordability improvements. Require persistence and avoid alert spam.
9. **Operations:** GitHub Actions or current UIP scheduler; retry/backoff, logs, health checks, daily run artifacts, failure notifications, secrets rotation, and source-cost controls.

## 9. Backtesting and acceptance gates
- Establish a single reproducible baseline with data vintages and NBER labels (recession target definitions explicitly documented).
- Evaluate RSI's lead/lag around recessions since ~1990, but avoid implying 1990 coverage for series unavailable then; restrict each test to valid coverage.
- Walk-forward probability calibration with Brier score, log loss, reliability, false alarm rates, precision/recall and lead time; compare to base-rate and benchmark yield-curve models.
- Backtest asset score against forward 6/12/24/36-month **risk-adjusted** returns, including drawdowns, realistic implementation assumptions, and asset availability (BTC/ETH do not exist for entire historical span).
- Backtest housing score by metro against affordability, mortgage payments, local price/rent outcomes, and buyer concessions where measured. No causal claims from starts alone.
- Unit tests: weight sum 1.0, bounded scores, arithmetic exactness, oil excluded from v2 RSI, no look-ahead, revision handling, stale suppression, empty data, and deterministic reruns.
- Integration tests: collector contracts, source manifests, publication gate, UI consistency, and no impact on portfolio transactions.
- **Acceptance:** a documented configuration, passing tests, 12+ months of reconstructed or observed comparable snapshots where data support it, explainable contributions, freshness alerts, and no automatic allocation changes. Calibration/backtests required before displaying numeric recession probability as a probability or labeling asset score as a buy recommendation.
